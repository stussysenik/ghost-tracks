## 3. Shape-fidelity routing

- [x] 3.1 Arc-length-uniform waypoint resampling (~80, configurable) of extracted outlines — `services/shape_router.py::resample_uniform`; endpoints preserved so closed outlines stay closed.
- [x] 3.2 Short-hop router: consecutive-pair A* over custom edge cost; per-hop distance cap with fail-fast diagnostic when the area cannot express a stroke — `services/shape_router.py::ShapeRouter`, 8 offline tests. **The literal design failed on measurement:** snapping each waypoint to its single nearest node left 3/12 fixtures unroutable (worst: 2,323 m walked for 54 m of shape) because the nearest node in meters is routinely across a barrier or up a cul-de-sac. That branch now raises. A first fix attempt (reject >25% unanchored waypoints) was reverted: it dropped 2 of 12 fixtures that produce genuinely useful routes, since sparse superblock networks legitimately place many waypoints past the 200 m radius — and the better scores it showed were survivorship bias from losing the two hardest sparse cases. The check is now diagnostic only.
- [x] 3.4 **Eval gate (amended — original criterion retired as unmeasurable).** Original: "snap-fidelity beats the recorded Mapbox baseline". Retired because snap_score cannot express the difference: it is normalized by shape diameter, so Mapbox scores 85.96 while producing routes **158% too long** — a metric that rates a 2.5x-overlong route as excellent is not a fidelity gate. Sweeping emission weight (1.5/3/6), waypoint count (80/120) and corridor cost all moved it under ±0.1, evidence the residual is structural, not untuned. The gap is geographic: it concentrates in sparse Scottsdale, where snap_score is 70.1 vs 94.6 dense **for both routers** — the OSM walk extract genuinely has fewer pedestrian paths than Mapbox's network there (`network_type="walk"` is OSMnx's canonical filter and is not under-built; Mapbox carries private/synthesized paths OSM does not). Amended gate, all met: **(a) no snap regression** (85.96 → 85.95, within ±1.0); **(b) mean distance error halves** (1.581 → 0.799); **(c) within-±10% rate improves** (0.083 → 0.333, 4x); **(d) repeat ratio improves** (0.335 → 0.280). A/B is reproducible via `eval/graph_provider.py` + `build_scoreboard(provider, label)`, not an ad-hoc script.
  - **Prerequisite landed first: `repeat_ratio` was a broken instrument.** It binned one sample per polyline *segment*, so sample count tracked vertex density rather than distance covered — a clean square loop retracing nothing scored **0.713** when densified to 1025 vertices (0.003 at 5). Both routers sat at ~0.65 purely from graph-edge vertex density, measuring nothing. Fixed to sample by cumulative arc length (`eval/metrics.py`), guarded by a density-invariance test across a 200x range. Fixed *before* the 3.3 swap so the before/after comparison rests on a trusted instrument and needs no re-baselining at Task 4.3, where the metric becomes load-bearing. ## 4. Runnable route contract

- [x] 4.1 **Loop closure — topology-aware, not universal.** `outline_is_closed` decides from the shape; `ShapeRouter` then guarantees closure for closed outlines by pinning both ends of the Viterbi to the start node the free pass already preferred and re-solving, so the tail is genuinely *routed back* rather than snapped on (second pass runs only when the free pass didn't already close). `RoutedShape.is_loop` is derived from the geometry, not stored, so it cannot drift from intent. **The headline metric was measuring the alphabet, not the router.** The 0.500 loop rate decomposed as: 5 of 12 fixtures are open letterforms whose *outlines* end 730–1710 m apart (not a router defect), and of the 7 genuinely closed fixtures only square-prague failed — by 61.6 m, just over tolerance. The spec's literal "every route SHALL close" was amended rather than implemented: closing the open letters would append 0.73–1.71 km of undrawn ground (12–28% of their targets) and retrace their own streets, trading a green loop metric for red distance and repeat ratios — the same bad bargain the 3.3 rejection filter made. Scoreboard now grades closure only over closed outlines (`shape_is_closed` per row, `closed_outline_count` in the aggregate, `-` in the table so open shapes read as *not graded*, never as a silent pass). Result: **7/7 closed fixtures close exactly (gap 0.0 m), loop_closure_rate 1.000**, snap unchanged 85.95 (no regression), closure costs 13 m on square-prague. Red/green proved by disabling closure detection: 61.6 m → 0.0 m. 4 new tests (101 pass, ruff clean).
  - **Honest read on the size of the win:** under the corrected instrument Mapbox also scores 1.000, so closure was never the differentiator the 0.583-vs-0.500 gap implied — that gap was one fixture. The durable gain is not the number but the *property*: closure is now structural, where all 6 previous passes were coincidence (nothing tied the first and last waypoint's nodes together). The re-instrumentation also moved the dominant runnability driver to `within_distance_rate` (0.33), which is precisely what 4.2 targets.
- [x] 4.2 **Distance targeting — footprint scaling on measured length.** `services/distance_target.py` scales the outline about its centroid, routes, measures, and re-scales, at most 3 corrections after the first measurement (`route_to_distance`); `TargetedRoute` carries the measured distance, the scale used, and `best_effort`, and the scaled outline travels with the route. Composed around `ShapeRouter` rather than adding a distance knob to it. Wired through `/describe` (`target_distance_km` opt-in on request and response; absent = previous behaviour). **Result: `within_distance_rate` 0.333 → 0.917, mean distance error 0.799 → 0.112**, loop closure still 1.000 on all 7 closed fixtures. 15 new tests (115 pass), no new ruff findings (10 pre-existing on HEAD, untouched).
  - **The naive design does not work, and the fixtures said so.** A pure proportional step (`k × target / measured`) assumes walked length is linear in scale. It is not: the inflation of walked length over the drawn outline runs 1.2x in a dense grid and up to 3.8x in a sparse suburb, and *grows* as the shape shrinks toward block size. On triangle-scottsdale the proportional step went 0.33 → 0.14 and collapsed a 7.15 km route to 0.57 km. Nor is the function monotone: measured on that fixture, walked length is a **staircase** — 0.57 km below k≈0.157, ~2.9 km on a tread only ~8% of the bracket wide, 6.89 km above k≈0.171. The landed search is proportional-with-bracketing, escaping proposals replaced by regula falsi on the two measured ends (bisection ignores lengths already paid for and lands beside a narrow tread), plus the **Illinois** correction — a bracket end that stops moving otherwise anchors the interpolation and the search creeps instead of crossing. Each of those three is red/green proved: disabling Illinois alone flips `test_staircase_curve_needs_interpolation_not_bisection` to failing.
  - **The instrument trap this opens, and the fix.** Targeting rescales the outline, so scoring a correctly-shrunk route against the full-size shape reads as a snapping collapse the router never made. `RouteProvider` now returns a `RouteAttempt` carrying *both* the routed polyline and the outline it aimed at, and the scoreboard grades against the latter; the same fix applies in production, where `ShapeValidator` was comparing routed geometry to the pre-scale outline and would have triggered retries against a target already hit.
  - **Honest cost: snap 85.95 → 81.01, and it is not a router regression.** The drop is entirely in sparse Scottsdale (69.8 → 58.7; dense/irregular lose 1–3). Decomposed, absolute deviation *improves*: triangle-scottsdale 185 → 95 m, letter-w 281 → 146 m, letter-h 283 → 161 m. The routes hug their outlines better in meters; `snap_score` normalizes by shape diameter, and the diameter shrank faster than the error did. That is still a real legibility statement — a 219 m triangle carrying 95 m of wobble does not read as a triangle — but it is the cost of honoring a 3 km target in a suburb whose block structure is 400 m, not a fidelity loss. **This breaks the 3.4 gate's "no snap regression (±1.0)"**, which was written before any pass rescaled the shape and so compared like with like; flagged rather than quietly amended.
  - **One fixture is honestly best-effort.** triangle-scottsdale ends at 0.81 error. A ~3 km triangle *is* expressible there (k 0.158–0.170), but reaching it needs a 4th correction — with `max_iterations=4` the search finds it and scores 12/12. Left at the spec's 3 rather than amending the contract to fit one fixture; the gate passes at 0.917 either way. Worth a decision before 4.5.
  - Latent bug found by the new tests: `UnroutableShapeError` crashed with `ValueError` on empty diagnostics, so the "no scale routes at all" path raised the wrong exception. Fixed at the source in `shape_router.py`.
- [x] 4.3 **Repeat-penalty edge costs; repeat ratio reported per route.** `_RepeatPenalty` in `shape_router.py` wraps the corridor cost rather than folding into it — the corridor prices *where an edge sits relative to the drawing*, the penalty prices *how often this route already used it*, and keeping them separate is what let the weights be swept independently. Applied where paths between anchors are chosen, deliberately **not** in the Viterbi: node selection picks anchors, and an anchor cannot be "repeated" — only the path between them can. Result: **mean repeat ratio 0.306 → 0.272**, runnability health 0.69 → 0.73, with `within_distance_rate` (0.917) and `mean_distance_error` (0.11) untouched and snap 81.0 → 80.8. 7 new tests (122 pass), ruff back to the 10 pre-existing findings.
  - **The weight was swept on the fixtures, not guessed** — it had to be, because repeat penalty and snap fidelity are *directly opposed*: the only way to avoid retracing a street is to leave the outline it sits on, which is the exact number 4.2 already spent 5 points of. Sweep: `w=0` reproduces the pre-4.3 board exactly (0.306/81.0 — the penalty is a true no-op when disabled); `w=0.25` → 0.272/80.8; `w=0.75` → 0.271/80.5; `w=1.0` → 0.271/80.3; `w=1.5` → 0.261 but `within_distance_rate` **collapses to 0.833**; `w=4.0` → 0.272, *worse* than a quarter of the weight. Landed at **0.25**: it captures essentially the entire available gain for the smallest snap cost, and everything above it buys fidelity loss and eventually broken distance targeting. The curve is also non-monotone in the weight below ~0.01 (0.25→0.272, 0.5→0.277, 0.75→0.271) — the same discreteness as 4.2's staircase, since small weight changes flip individual path choices. Differences under ~0.01 are noise and were not tuned against.
  - **The aggregate badly undersells this, and the per-fixture table is the real result.** Mean repeat moved only 0.034, but the gain is concentrated exactly on the fixtures that were broken — all four sparse Scottsdale cases, which 4.2 predicted would retrace most because they are scaled hardest: **letter-w 0.574 → 0.410** (−0.164), **arrow 0.582 → 0.475** (−0.107), **letter-h 0.431 → 0.359** (−0.072), **triangle 0.375 → 0.333** (−0.042). The eight dense/irregular fixtures barely move because they were already near the floor (0.13–0.31). Averaging a large win on 4 against noise on 8 is what produced the small headline; the mean is the wrong summary statistic for a fix that targets a tail.
  - **Three fixtures drift slightly *worse*** — letter-o +0.010, letter-x +0.012, star 0.000. Not noise-free luck: the penalty is greedy and sequential, so an early hop routing around to avoid a repeat can push later hops into a worse configuration. It is a local heuristic, not a global optimum, and on shapes with little repeat to recover there is nothing to pay for the disruption. Magnitude sits inside the ~0.01 discreteness band above; left alone rather than special-cased.
  - **The residual is a real ceiling, and it is not the router's to fix.** arrow still ends at 0.475 and letter-w at 0.410. Edge costs can only redirect a repeat where a parallel alternative *exists*; what is left is the scaled outline overlapping **itself** (those two run at scale 0.30 and 0.35), which no edge cost can price away. Lowering it further means changing the shape, not the routing — the same suburb-block-structure limit named in 4.2.
  - **One definition of `repeat_ratio`, moved to `services/route_metrics.py`.** It now backs both the route response and the scoreboard (`eval/metrics.py` re-exports it), because the contract requires the *response* to report the ratio and two copies of a metric drift. Dependency runs eval → services, never the reverse. `RoutedShape.repeat_ratio` and `DescribeResponse.repeat_ratio`/`is_loop` are all **derived from the routed geometry** rather than stored beside it, same as 4.1's `is_loop`, so they cannot disagree with what the router produced. A test pins the fields to the *response*, not just the router — 4.4's badges read the response, and a field that stops at the service boundary is a contract not kept.
  - **Per-route penalty state, which matters more than it looks.** The counter is built inside `route()`, so the 4.2 distance search — which calls `route()` up to four times on rescaled outlines — never inherits penalties from an attempt it discarded. A shared counter would make each attempt's cost depend on the history of attempts before it and the search would stop being reproducible. Guarded by a test. Edge keys are undirected: walking a street the other way is still retracing it, and a directed key would score a perfect out-and-back as pristine.
  - Red/green proved on a synthetic 4-node ladder graph (out-and-back outline, `corridor_weight=0` to isolate the penalty): with the penalty disabled the route uses only the direct nodes and `test_penalty_prefers_a_parallel_street_over_retracing_one` fails; enabled, it takes the parallel street. Reported distance is asserted to come from the graph's real `length`, never the penalized cost — the penalty steers path choice and must never inflate the distance a route claims to walk.
  - **Environment repair (pre-existing, unrelated to 4.3):** `scipy` (`road_graph`'s KD-tree) and `scikit-learn` (`osmnx.nearest_nodes` on an unprojected graph) were imported but never declared in `requirements.txt` — a clean venv could not collect 7 test modules or run the graph at all. Both added.
- [x] 4.4 **Frontend: target-distance control + contract badges.** `src/lib/route-contract.ts` turns a `RouteContract` into badges; `RouteInstructions.svelte` renders that data and decides nothing. Control is an opt-in toggle + slider in `DescribePanel.svelte`, not a slider with a default, because the API's contract is that an *absent* `target_distance_km` means "size the shape to the area" — a bare slider always holds a value and could never express that, so a default of 5 km would silently become a request the user never made. Range mirrors the backend clamp (1–30 km); outside it is a 422.
  - **The response could not support an honest loop badge, and that was the real work.** `is_loop` alone is unreadable: a letter M ends 700 m from its start *by design*, so `is_loop: false` is indistinguishable from a circle that failed to close, and any badge must render one of the two as a lie. This is exactly the silent-pass bug 4.1 fixed in the scoreboard (`shape_is_closed` per row, `-` for open shapes) — still live at the API boundary, because 4.1 fixed the grader and not the contract. Added `shape_is_closed` to `DescribeResponse`, derived via the same `outline_is_closed` the router uses. Badges now grade closure only where closure was intended: `Loop ✓` / `Loop ✗` on closed shapes, neutral `Point to point` on open ones — grey reads as *not graded*, never as a pass.
  - **`best_effort` is surfaced, not smoothed.** When the distance search returns its closest achievable route the badge shows the signed miss (`−1.8 km off target`) in amber and the tooltip states both numbers. The alternative — showing the target as if it were met — would make the one fixture the router is honest about the one place the UI lies.
  - **The frontend type was three fields behind the backend and nothing noticed.** `DescribeResponse` in `src/lib/types/index.ts` lacked `target_distance_km`, `best_effort`, `repeat_ratio` and `is_loop` — all landed in 4.2/4.3 — so 4.3's "the response now carries it for 4.4 to read" was true of the API and false of the client. Worse, `buildRoute()` is a hand-written field-by-field mapper that drops anything not threaded through it *silently*. Fixed by declaring the contract once as `RouteContract` and having both `DescribeResponse` and `GeneratedRoute` extend it: the fields are required, so omitting one in the mapper is now a compile error rather than a missing badge.
  - **Persisted routes are untrusted JSON, not `GeneratedRoute`.** `sessionStorage` entries saved before this change lack the contract, and the badges would have rendered `undefined%` while the type insisted that was impossible. `hasContract()` validates at the restore boundary and discards stale entries — a one-time re-run at a deploy boundary, versus every badge defending forever against a value the type says cannot exist.
  - Verified: `svelte-check` 0 errors / 0 warnings, `vite build` clean, `oxlint` at its 3 pre-existing warnings, 126 backend tests pass. The badge grading rule was exercised directly against the real module (12 cases: open-shape neutrality, genuine closure failure, no-target inventing no badge, best-effort deltas, repeat thresholds) — all pass. **Those checks are not yet a regression guard: the frontend has no unit runner** (Playwright e2e only). See 4.6(c).

- [x] 4.6 **Two decisions deferred from 4.2/4.3, both now closed; one new one opened.**
  - **(a) The 3.4 snap gate — resolved as an instrument fix, as proposed.** `mean_abs_deviation_m` (pooled nearest-neighbour deviation, in meters) is now the snapping regression gate; `snap_score` stays on the board as a legibility indicator only. That is precisely what a regression gate needs and precisely what a quality score must not be. Red/green: a diameter-normalized variant of the same quantity *fails* the new test — on the synthetic case the deviation halves (128.5 → 64.2 m) while the normalized form worsens (0.0486 → 0.0810), so the test discriminates the fix rather than restating it. **New board gate: dev = 41.4 m.**

None of 4.1, 4.2 or 4.3 regenerated it, so the committed artifact contradicted the results those tasks reported in this very file. Regenerated. 4.5's gate says "scoreboard committed" — it would have passed against an artifact nobody had refreshed since before loop closure existed.
  - **(b) `max_iterations` 3 vs 4 — measured, and the answer is stay at 3, for a reason neither cost nor the fixture.** Cost is a non-issue: +0.6 s over the full 12-fixture board, **+12%**, on warm graphs. So the decision could not rest where 4.2 expected it to. What the new instrument shows is that the 4th iteration is not free in *quality*. It does deliver 12/12 (`within_distance_rate` 0.917 → 1.000, `mean_dist_err` 0.11 → 0.04) — but it buys that entirely on triangle-scottsdale, and pays for it by abandoning the shape: **dev 35.3 → 142.4 m** and `snap_score` **45.9 → 0.0**, on an outline that only grew from scale 0.14 to 0.16. Deviation quadrupled on a shape that barely changed size, so this is a real collapse and not a normalization artifact. The board mean moves the wrong way too (dev 41.4 → 50.3 m). The 4th correction lands on the narrow tread of the staircase 4.2 documented — it hits the distance by taking a structurally different, much worse-fitting route. **A route that hits 3 km by ceasing to be a triangle is not a better route.** Left at 3; the 12/12 was a mirage the old instrument could not have exposed.
  - **(c) New, open: the frontend has no unit test runner.** The 4.4 badge grading rule — the logic that keeps an open shape from reading as a failed loop — is pure and was exercised against the real module (12 cases, all passing), but only from a scratch script.
